# Ep 2 · Why every wormhole slams shut (Sun Nov 1, 12 PM CT)

Every wormhole Einstein's math allows... slams shut. Why?

Hey, it's Lola. Last week we found a bridge hidden in Einstein's equations. This week, let's find out why we can't use it.

Picture the bridge as a tunnel with a narrow middle. Physicists call it the throat. In 1962, John Wheeler and Robert Fuller worked out what that throat does over time. It opens... and pinches shut again, so fast that not even light can make it through. Anything that steps inside gets crushed.

The reason is gravity. Everything we know, matter, light, energy, pulls. And that pull squeezes the throat closed. To hold it open, you'd need something that pushes. Physicists call it exotic matter: stuff with negative energy.

In 1988, Kip Thorne and his student Mike Morris took that idea seriously. Carl Sagan had asked Thorne for help with his novel, Contact. So they asked: what would a wormhole you could actually travel through need? Their answer: a throat propped open by exotic matter. Einstein's math allows it, if that matter exists.

In sci-fi, the ring just stays open. No exotic matter required.

So here's my question: Is negative energy even real? Or did we just invent it to make the math behave?

Next Sunday: it's real. And we've measured it. Follow along, we're doing this together.
